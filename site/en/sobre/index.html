About

From Inhumas to the world. And back to Goiás.

I'm Gabriel Rodrigues. I was born in Inhumas, Goiás, in 1993, and I've worked in software for more than ten years, eight of them in Europe.

I came back to Goiás in 2026. I want to bring into politics what I learned fixing systems: find where they get stuck, measure, and change things carefully.

Age 12 · Countryside of Goiás

Change in my head

I come from a simple family. We sold coxinha and pastel, Brazilian fried snacks, from one town festival to the next, and lived month to month.

I started working at 12. I was good at giving change and remembered everyone's order by heart.

My mother still makes snacks and sells them at the Sunday market in Inhumas. My brother has a stall there too.

I bought my first computer with money from collecting cans and from my pay as a young apprentice, bagging groceries at a supermarket. Before that, it was the internet café.

As a teenager, I set up a server for an online game that reached 500 people playing at the same time. I worked on the code, built the website and hunted cheaters.

2014 to 2018 · Goiânia

Who is on the other side of the screen

In 2014 I started a degree in Systems Analysis and Development at PUC Goiás, paid with FIES, the federal student loan. That same year I started working at a software company in Goiânia.

I built the web version of a debt collection system, so people in debt could negotiate without a high-pressure phone call. One day, while testing it, I found a familiar name on the list of debtors. It was someone in my family.

It's hard to see your family needing money and not be able to help. I'll tell that story in full someday, calmly. Since that day, before I build any system, I think about who is on the other side.

Back then I also answered programming questions on Stack Overflow in Portuguese, a questions and answers site. I became one of the top 50 contributors in the site's history. I like explaining things, and that's what I try to do on this site.

2017 to 2026 · Malta and Spain

Eight years away

As a junior developer I earned R$ 2,500 a month. My FIES debt was over R$ 60,000. At that pace, I would only finish paying it in 2032.

I went to work in Malta with a plan and a date to come back: learn English, pay off FIES and help my family. There I saw the sea for the first time, in 2017, at 24. The first thing I did was taste the water. It really is salty.

I went thinking I would work on video games. I ended up at online betting companies. I know that weighs on people, so I'll say it plainly.

I chose the part of the work that protects players: checking identity, limiting how much a person can deposit and lose, blocking people who ask to stop playing and raising alerts when money looks like laundering.

In that job, one bug can let someone go back to betting after asking to stop.

The turning point

Why politics

The first company I worked for in Europe was bought by a bigger one. Bit by bit, the care for players was cut along with it.

Then I watched Brazil legalize betting without serious tools against abuse, while European countries were tightening their rules. Here, I saw influencers selling bets as if they were investments.

Inside a company, the people who handle protection lose when profit speaks louder and nobody gets punished. Good code doesn't beat a bad rule. And rules change in politics.

2020 to 2026 · From afar, then up close

Why I came back

In the middle of the pandemic, my parents were in a car accident. I was on the other side of the ocean. I sold all my stocks to help.

My parents are getting older, and I want to be near them. I came back to Goiás in 2026. I also can't watch the place where I was born suffer from problems I know can be solved.

Faith and knowledge

What I believe

I was baptized in an evangelical church as a teenager, when I was lost. I believe in God, and I believe it is up to us to take care of our lives and seek knowledge together, with religion or without.

In practice

How I work

  1. Find the cause. Repeating a fix that already failed doesn't help. First I investigate and measure.
  2. Speak clearly to the people affected. In software, they're the users. In government, they're the public.
  3. Change a little and check. One small, measured step, then the next.

One example. In 2025 I bought an apartment in Goiânia and paid about R$ 10,000 in tax and notary fees. I tracked down every real. In Brazil, putting a property in your name takes 14 steps and 31 days, according to the World Bank. In Georgia, one step and one day. That became the digital registry proposal.

The hard question

Why trust me?

I spent eight years abroad. I have no office and no party. Trust has to be earned, and I know it.

I also know that people in software tend to be bad at communication. Me included. That's why I chose to learn in public.

I don't want a term spent pointing fingers. I want to understand each system together with the people who live the problem, and improve what can be improved.

My goal is to run for federal deputy for Goiás in 2030. Until then, every idea stays on this site, with sources and open to criticism.
