Idea

Brazil Answers: hearing from the people who live the problem before voting on the law

Asked directly, 4% of Brazilian women say they suffered violence at home. With the right question, it is 33%. That is what the Senate's research institute, DataSenado, found in 2025 after interviewing 21,641 women. Many do not see themselves as victims until someone describes the situation. The question changes the answer. And the answer should change the law.

Brazil Answers (Brasil Responde) is a simple idea: before voting on a law, Congress sends a questionnaire, through gov.br, the government's login system, to the people who live the problem. They answer anonymously. If they want, they can give their name and send a video telling what they went through.

Chapter 1 · A 2026 case

The 6-day work week was voted on without knowing who would be hit hardest

In May 2026, Brazil's lower house approved the end of the "6x1" schedule, six days of work for one day off, with a maximum of 40 hours a week (constitutional amendment PEC 221/2019). It is a big change for people who work six days to rest one.

Two weeks before the vote, the chair of the special committee said it was still undecided whether companies would get any compensation or a transition period. The business data cited in the hearing was a survey by Sebrae, the small business agency, of 8,000 entrepreneurs. There was no public study, sector by sector, of who would be hit hardest.

A neighborhood bakery, a supermarket and a factory do not feel the change the same way. To protect jobs, the government needs to know where to help. And to understand the law, a congressman needs to hear from the mother who has no day to spend with her kids.

Chapter 2 · How it works today

The channels exist, but each one is in its own corner

  1. 1
    e-Cidadania, from the SenateAnyone can suggest a law. With 20,000 supporters in four months, the idea becomes a suggestion for senators. In public consultations, each person votes yes or no on a bill.
    Stuck: the vote does not say who is affected, and it is not a sample of the country
  2. 2
    e-Democracia, from the lower houseYou can send questions during live hearings and comment on the text of bills.
    Stuck: only people who already follow Congress take part
  3. 3
    Brasil Participativo, from the federal governmentIn 2023, 1.4 million people signed up to vote on proposals for the government's four-year plan, the PPA.
    Stuck: it serves the government's plan, not the laws of Congress

All three hear from whoever shows up. None of them goes looking for the people who live the problem, with questions written for that group.

Chapter 3 · The numbers

The structure already exists

77.8 miBrazilians have a gold gov.br account, the one with the most verified identity. There are about 172 million users in total. Agência Gov, December 2025
36e-Cidadania ideas became bills, out of 221 that reached senators as suggestions. Agência Senado, 2024
76.5%of the participatory PPA proposals that fit the plan were accepted by the government, fully or partly. Presidency's General Secretariat, 2023

Listening to people works when the question reaches the people who decide. What is missing is to connect gov.br, which knows who a person is, to the laws, which need to know what that person lives.

Chapter 4 · What I propose

From the debate to the questionnaire, and back to the law

  1. 1
    The hearing becomes questionsThe committee holds the usual public hearing. An artificial intelligence transcribes everything and gathers the points of supporters and critics. At the end, it proposes three versions of a questionnaire.
  2. 2
    Lawmakers choose, and a research agency checksThe committee votes on which version goes out. Before it does, IBGE, the national statistics agency, or another research body reviews the questions so they do not push an answer. All three versions are made public.
  3. 3
    Each person sees questionnaires for their profilePeople log in through gov.br and see only what applies to them: formal employees, business owners, women, retirees, residents of a region. They choose whether to answer.
  4. 4
    Two doors: anonymous or with a nameOn the anonymous one, the answer is stored without the person's tax ID (CPF). On the other, the person gives their name and can send a link to a video of themselves on YouTube or Instagram, telling what they went through.
  5. 5
    Two samples, side by sideAnyone in the profile can answer. On top of that, a group is drawn at random and invited, the way IBGE runs surveys. The report shows both. If an organized campaign floods the open sample, the gap shows up.
  6. 6
    The report goes with the billLawmakers still cast the vote. But they vote knowing who is affected, where it hurts most and what people are asking for.

How it would work with the 6-day week

For workers

How many days off do you get a month? Do you have a second job? Who looks after your kids on Saturday? If your days off doubled, what would change in your life?

For employers

How many people do you employ? Do you open on Sunday? How much of your costs is payroll? If you had to hire more, could you? What help would make a difference?

With that, the report would show the sectors and company sizes under the most pressure. Transition help would go to them, instead of one rule for everyone.

Chapter 5 · Protecting the people who answer

Truly anonymous, not just in name

People who answer about violence at home or about their boss take a risk. That is why protection comes before any question.

Anonymous answer
  • gov.br only confirms three things: this is a real person, in the right profile, who has not answered yet. Then it hands out a single-use pass.
  • The answer is stored in a separate system, with no CPF, no name and no exact time. Not even the government can link the answer to the person.
  • Very small groups do not appear in the report, so no one can be identified by elimination.
Sensitive topics
  • The questionnaire does not stay in the app's history and has a quick exit button.
  • Logging in requires face recognition and a second factor. If the account is opened on a new device, the owner is alerted.
  • At the end, the person gets the help lines, such as Ligue 180, Brazil's hotline for women.
  • To be studied with the National Data Protection Authority: with the account owner's prior permission, keep a photo of anyone who tries to get in without her, to serve as evidence.
Video testimony
  • It is always optional and only for people who answered with their name.
  • The video does not count toward the numbers. It shows the face of the problem, and the numbers show its size.
  • A team checks the video before attaching it to the report. The person can withdraw it at any time.

Brazil's data protection law (LGPD) treats health and sex life data as sensitive. It allows their use in research and public policy, preferably without identifying anyone. Data that can no longer be linked to a person stops being personal data (LGPD, articles 11 and 12).

Chapter 6 · Abroad

Who already does something similar

Taiwan: the debate that finds consensus

Since 2015, vTaiwan has used an open program, Pol.is, to show where groups that disagree actually agree. The first test was about how to regulate Uber. Civil servants from the agency in charge are required to take part.

Barcelona: open-source participation

The city created Decidim, an open platform for residents to propose and vote. Today it is used by governments in several countries.

Neither combines verified identity, profile and a random sample. Brazil has the piece they are missing: a gov.br with 172 million users.

Chapter 7 · Who does what

Start small, in one committee

First step
  • A pilot in the lower house, created by resolution, in one committee and on one or two bills.
  • gov.br is only the front door, through an agreement with the federal government. The questions and the data stay with Congress.
If it works
  • A law to make Brazil Answers permanent, in both houses of Congress, connected to e-Cidadania and e-Democracia.
  • Clear rules: who writes the questions, who reviews them, how the random draw works and how data is deleted.
  • Penalties for anyone who fires or harasses someone for having answered.

Why in Congress and not in the government? Because Congress writes the laws. And because the government of the day should not control the questions that guide the laws.

Chapter 8 · The criticism

The hardest questions

These are the toughest questions I would expect to hear, and my answers.

There is already e-Cidadania, e-Democracia and Brasil Participativo. Why one more?

They hear from whoever shows up, with a yes or no vote. They do not ask the people affected, and they do not separate the boss from the employee.

Brazil Answers does not need to be a new website. It can be a feature inside what already exists, with profiles and a random sample.

This is an internet poll. The people who answer are the angry ones with free time.

That is why there are two samples. The random one follows IBGE's method and represents the group. The open one shows who wanted to speak.

Many people choose a politician out of anger at the other side. Data from real people who live the problem helps get past that.

Organized groups and activists will take over.

gov.br blocks fake accounts and repeat votes. Real activists are real people, and they have the right to answer. But if the open sample is very different from the random one, the report shows it.

And the questionnaire decides nothing. If a majority asks for something the data shows is harmful, the lawmaker is still free to vote against it and explain why.

The abuser can grab her phone and see what she answered.

This is the most serious risk, and there is no easy fix. That is why the sensitive questionnaire does not stay in the history, has a quick exit and requires face recognition.

If the account is opened on a new device, the owner is alerted. This needs to be tested with people who support women victims of violence before it goes live.

A video online exposes the woman. And it could be fake, made with AI.

The video is optional, the person can withdraw it at any time and it does not count toward the numbers. A team checks it before attaching it.

These days you cannot trust everything that circulates. But it is better to have data and filter it than to have none.

Employees will not tell the truth for fear of being fired.

On the anonymous door, the answer has no CPF. Neither the government nor the boss can know who answered, and small groups do not appear in the report.

And the Brazil Answers law needs to punish anyone who fires or harasses someone for taking part.

Whoever writes the question controls the answer.

True, and that is why the process is public. The AI proposes three versions based on the hearing, lawmakers choose, and a research agency checks that the question is neutral.

All three versions are published. Anyone can see what was dropped and why.

Nobody will answer that they want to pay more taxes.

No. That is why the questionnaire is not a referendum. It does not ask "do you approve?", it asks what the person lives and what would change for them.

Each case needs a careful reading, and the decision stays with the people who were elected.

Chapter 9 · How to measure

Four numbers to track

  • Bills with a report: how many bills voted on had a questionnaire first.
  • Changes to the text: how many articles were changed because of what people answered.
  • Gap between the samples: how much the open sample differs from the random one, topic by topic.
  • Leaks: zero cases of an anonymous answer linked to a person. If one happens, the program stops until it is fixed.
