A Brazilian car that everyone paints their own way

The Dumont Challenge (Desafio Dumont) is a bill proposal. It opens a national competition for teams to create a simple, modular, easy-to-customize car. The starting money comes from people with large fortunes who choose to invest in the challenge instead of paying part of their tax. The repair shop in your neighborhood gets the work.

Chapter 1 · The gap

Brazil sells 14,000 Fiat Stradas a month and almost no electric pickups

In October 2026, almost 1 in 4 cars sold in Brazil was already electric or hybrid. But for work vehicles, the small pickup and the van, the picture changes: only 3% were electrified. No Brazilian automaker competes in that space.

And there's something almost nobody notices: more than 80% of new cars leave the factory white, black or gray. A country as colorful as ours drives colorless cars.

Chapter 2 · How it works today

How a foreign carmaker gets in, and where a Brazilian one gets stuck

The rules apply to everyone. A foreign automaker arriving in Brazil almost always follows the same script, and uses the same incentives a Brazilian one could use.

  1. 1
    Import and test the marketChery imported from 2009 onwards. BYD started selling cars here in 2022.
  2. 2
    Assemble in a factory that already existsBYD took over the old Ford plant in Camaçari (Bahia). GWM, the old Mercedes plant in Iracemápolis (São Paulo).
    Bottleneck for a Brazilian company: no parent company to fund this phase
  3. 3
    Use the incentivesMOVER research credit (the federal auto industry program), the Lei do Bem R&D tax break and state ICMS tax credits. Goiás returns 92.5% to 93.3% of ICMS to automakers until 2032.
    Bottleneck: the MOVER credit is calculated on revenue, and the Lei do Bem only applies under the actual-profit tax regime. Whoever isn't selling yet is left out.
  4. 4
    Localize little by littleMore parts from here, more engineering from here. Chery opened a factory in 2014. BYD has been assembling in Bahia since July 2025.

And for those who want to customize their car, the rules get in the way too. Changing the structure of a registered car requires prior authorization (Traffic Code, art. 98). Wrapping more than half the car counts as a color change on the registration document (Contran Resolution 916/2022).

Chapter 3 · Where the idea comes from

Copy what works. Improve what doesn't.

Slate, in the United States

An electric pickup with no paint and no dashboard screen. Color comes from a wrap, and a kit turns the pickup into a five-seat SUV. It raised about US$ 1.4 billion and passed 100,000 reservations in three weeks (2025). The price went from "under US$ 20,000" to US$ 24,950 (2026).

Gurgel, in Brazil

It made more than 40,000 vehicles and exported to dozens of countries. It went under when its people's car depended on a lower tax, and that lower tax was given to all 1.0-liter cars in 1990. Lesson: you can't depend on a single benefit.

The name comes from Santos Dumont, the Brazilian aviation pioneer. In 1910 he published the drawings of the Demoiselle, his light airplane, for free. Anyone could build one. The challenge follows the same idea: the kits' fitting dimensions are public, so any repair shop or small factory can create accessories.

To remember what went wrong: between 2012 and 2017, the Inovar-Auto program raised the IPI excise tax by 30 points for those who didn't use Brazilian parts. The WTO condemned the program at the request of Japan and the European Union. The Dumont Challenge does the opposite: it doesn't punish imports, it rewards those who invent here.

Chapter 4 · What I propose

From an open challenge to a factory

The Dumont Challenge uses the same funnel as the FSRI. Nobody picks the winner out of friendship. And I'm not part of any team.

  1. 1
    National challengeThe call asks for a simple, modular, safe and easy-to-repair car. The technical panel sets the safety, cost and performance targets. Teams from all over the country sign up with a prototype, technology and business model.
  2. 2
    Acceleration and public panelThe best ones receive R$ 200,000 to R$ 500,000 and mentoring at universities. Then they pitch live to engineers, experts and investors. The public also votes, for a separate prize.
  3. 3
    Private investment, released by milestonesThe big money comes, through the FSRI, from people with large fortunes who pay little tax today. If that's not enough, it opens to ordinary investors. Each part is only released when a technical milestone is met.
  4. 4
    Manufacture and localize at the same timeStart where possible: an idle line in another factory or a new factory in a hub with a skilled workforce, such as Anápolis and Catalão, in Goiás. Invest where we don't have capacity yet, like batteries, which becomes another challenge.
  5. 5
    A network of repair shops across BrazilThe automaker certifies repair shops to install kits and apply wraps. The shop guarantees its work. The color is up to each owner.

What the law changes

Brazilian Modular Vehicle Law
  • Creates the Dumont Challenge within Tier 3 of the FSRI, with a cap in reais on public insurance per project.
  • Factory-approved kit, installed in the neighborhood. Amends art. 98 of the Traffic Code: a kit certified together with the car can be installed by an accredited shop, which issues the certificate and updates the registration. The model already exists for natural gas (CNG) conversions, with installers registered at Inmetro and accredited inspection.
  • Custom color on the registration document, for wraps and colored panels, without an inspection at every change.
  • Research credit for those without revenue yet. A new company receives the MOVER and Lei do Bem credit before it sells, as a credit it can use later or sell.
  • Open standard. Whoever receives money from the fund publishes the kits' fitting dimensions.
  • Conflict rule. Politicians and their relatives can't be on a team or invest.
Already exists, just use it
  • The Consumer Protection Code already holds the automaker liable for defects (art. 12) and the shop for its service (art. 14). When an installed part causes harm, both are jointly liable (art. 25).
  • Public procurement of innovation, under Brazil's Startup Legal Framework, lets city governments and state-owned companies buy the first cars for their fleets.
Press the government
  • The zero IPI tax of the Sustainable Car program requires painting done in Brazil. A car with color-molded or wrapped panels needs to fit that rule. That's a decree, not a law.
Chapter 5 · Where the money comes from

Tax that turns into a factory

A new car takes billions. Caoa Chery announced R$ 3 billion for Anápolis. BYD, R$ 5.5 billion for Camaçari. At the same time, Brazil's 255 billionaires hold R$ 1.86 trillion, equivalent to 14.6% of GDP (Forbes, 2026).

Chapter 6 · The criticism

The hardest questions

These are the toughest questions I'd expect to hear, and my answers.

Are you going to start a car company?

No. I propose a law that opens a challenge. The car and the company are created by the winning teams.

I don't join any team, I don't invest and I recuse myself from any decision of the fund.

Can R$ 500,000 make a car? That costs billions.

R$ 500,000 makes a prototype and a business plan, not a factory. The challenge has phases.

The big money only comes after the public panel, from private investment and by milestones. Slate, in the United States, needed about US$ 1.4 billion. That's the scale we're talking about, and it doesn't come out of the government's coffers.

If it fails, do taxpayers pay?

In Tier 3 of the FSRI, public insurance covers at most half of the loss, and only in an audited bankruptcy. The investor always loses the bigger part.

The law sets a cap in reais per project. And since the money is released by milestones, the risk at any moment is only what has already been released.

Isn't this Inovar-Auto, which the WTO condemned?

No. Inovar-Auto raised taxes on those who didn't use Brazilian parts. The WTO accepts support for research. It does not accept charging different taxes depending on whether a part is local or imported.

The Dumont Challenge doesn't raise anyone's taxes. It funds research, prototypes and patents in Brazil.

The battery comes from China. Is that sovereignty?

It's starting where we can. Even BYD assembles batteries in Bahia with cells from China.

A car with most of its parts from here is better than none. A Brazilian battery cell becomes another challenge of the fund, because it takes more time and more money.

BYD sells an electric car for R$ 119,000. How do you compete?

Not on price alone. On the work vehicle that barely exists in electric form today, on modularity and on the network of shops that customize.

And on the security of not depending only on others. Exchange rates, tariffs and the 2021 chip shortage have already stopped factories all over the world.

Who guarantees the shop won't make an unsafe car?

The shop only installs kits already approved together with the car, and must be certified by the automaker. It's the CNG conversion model, which has worked for years with registered installers and inspection.

If something goes wrong, the consumer can already sue the shop and the automaker together, under the Consumer Protection Code.

Why would idle factories rent a line to a competitor?

Because an idle line loses money. Factories all over the world assemble other brands' cars under contract. If nobody rents, the other option is a dedicated factory in a hub with a ready workforce.

Doesn't giving credit to companies with no revenue open the door to shell companies?

It would, if the money came out all at once. That's why the credit is only released against an audited milestone: prototype ready, crash test done, certification approved.

A company that sold reservations without a car was suspended from MOVER in 2026. The rule has to catch that earlier.

Isn't assembling first and localizing later what the Chinese do?

Yes, and it works. That's why we copy it. The difference is that here the engineering, the patent and the company are Brazilian from day one. We manufacture and localize at the same time.

Chapter 7 · How to measure

Four numbers to track

  • Electric light commercial vehicles sold per month, per Fenabrave.
  • Brazilian parts in the winning car, as a % of cost, year by year.
  • Certified repair shops per state and jobs created in them.
  • Public money at risk, in reais, published every month by the fund.
