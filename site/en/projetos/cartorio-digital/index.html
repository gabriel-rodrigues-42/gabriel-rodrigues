Under study · FSRI Tier 1

Putting a property in your name in Brazil takes 14 steps. It could take one.

In 2025 I bought a R$ 250,000 apartment in Goiânia and paid about R$ 10,000 in taxes and notary fees. I set out to understand where each real went, and how other countries do the same thing in a day.

Today: certificates, deed, tax and registration, each in a different placeProposal: one counter, everything checked

Chapter 1 · Where the money goes

Half is tax. Half is the notary office.

In Brazil, property transfers go through cartórios, private notary and registry offices run under public concession. When people talk about "notary fees", they mix three things: the ITBI, a city tax on property transfers; the emolumentos, which are the notary's price; and the transfers to state funds that come built into that price. Pick a value and see the split in Goiânia, using the official 2026 fee table.

Property value

5.0% of the property value, not counting certificates
  • ITBI (2%), city tax
  • State funds and ISS service tax on registration*
  • Deed at the notary office
  • Property registry, filing and title record

* The funds and ISS on registration are estimated at 29.25% of the fee, based on the split published for Goiânia's 1st Property Registry. The official table doesn't show the funds on the deed, so it may cost more than shown here. By law, 40% of notary fees in Goiás go to 11 public funds and bodies.

Chapter 2 · How it works today

Four counters, lots of certificates

In Brazil, ownership only passes to you once the registration is done (Civil Code, art. 1,245). Until then, this is the path:

  1. 1
    Gather certificatesOn the seller and the property: liens, lawsuits, debts, marital status. Each one comes from a different place.
    Bottleneck: paperwork scattered across many registries
  2. 2
    Sign the deedAt the notary office, in person or by video call since 2020. Mandatory for property worth more than 30 minimum wages (Civil Code, art. 108).
    Bottleneck: only a notary from the property's or the buyer's state counts
  3. 3
    Pay the ITBIAt city hall. In Goiânia, 2% of the higher of the price and the assessed value.
  4. 4
    RegisterAt the area's property registry. The registrar checks everything again and only then opens the new page of the title record.
    Bottleneck: price by bracket, with built-in transfers

The technology already exists. The sector itself says 42% of notarial acts were electronic in 2024. But a deed by video follows the same fee table as the counter, and there is almost no competition.

Chapter 4 · How other countries do it

The shortcut is integration, not reinvention

3 steps · 17.5 days · 0.5%, with a mandatory notary
Brazil
14 steps · 31 days · 3.6%

Estonia shows you can have notaries and still be cheap. What makes the difference is integrated data and the process in a single place.

Portugal: the State competes

The public Casa Pronta counter handles the purchase and the registration in a single visit, alongside private notaries. When Portugal reformed its notary system in 2004, it gave a two-year transition and let each notary choose their path.

Romania: the 2026 scare

In July 2026, an attacker wiped the property registry database and froze almost every sale in the country. What saved it were copies kept in separate places. No system is unbreakable; the defense is backups, audit trails and recovery.

What about blockchain? In Georgia, it only stores a "fingerprint" of each record to prove nobody tampered with it later. In Sweden it stayed a prototype, and in Honduras it stalled before the pilot. In Brazil, the Central Bank switched off the Drex blockchain platform in 2025. That's why the proposal talks about a history that can't be erased, with whatever technology is safest and most auditable.

Chapter 5 · What I propose

A simple path for the simple case

Most sales look alike: the property is paid off, with no debt and no dispute. For that case, the proposal is a single path, at a public counter, without closing any notary office by decree.

  1. 1
    Both go to Vapt VuptGoiás's one-stop public service centers: 78 units and about 620,000 visits a month (Goiás Government, 2026). They already issue ID cards and driver's licenses there, with photo and fingerprints.
  2. 2
    The system checks on its ownDebts, liens, freezes, marital status and ITBI show up on screen, cross-checking databases that already exist. Nobody chases certificates.
  3. 3
    A trained person talks to the sellerIf the seller is elderly or vulnerable, there is a questionnaire to check that the sale is freely made. The buyer digitally signs off on all relevant facts.
  4. 4
    The record goes into a history that can't be erasedEvery change is stored with an audit trail and copies in several places, including offline. Both leave with a one-page summary.

Who does what

Goiás can do it now
  • An agreement so Vapt Vupt becomes a service point of the official system that already exists.
  • Public procurement of innovation, under Brazil's Startup Legal Framework, for the software that checks the simple case and integrates the cities' ITBI. Open innovation marathon, public judging panel, State-owned and auditable code.
  • Review the 40% in transfers, which are state law, and show on the receipt how much goes where.
Needs federal law
  • Allow a paid-off, debt-free property to be transferred through the simple path, without a deed, as already happens with the bank contract in a mortgage.
  • Require publishing, office by office, how much each notary office collects.
  • Public oversight of the registry's digital systems.
Only with a constitutional amendment
  • A state-run property registry, if that is ever the way. The transition would be gradual, as offices become vacant, state by state, without breaking the rights of those who passed the public exam.
Chapter 6 · A real case

The scam the notary office catches too late

In 2026, Jornal da Record, a Brazilian TV news show, reported a case from Goiás. An elderly man believed he had bought a farm. When he went to register it, the document was fake, and he was the one arrested for using a forged document. The Civil Police then investigated, and the scammers were arrested in Goiânia.

It is not an isolated case. The Goiás Civil Police itself has published others:

R$ 4 mnwas the value of the farm in Nova Crixás that a man tried to transfer with a forged power of attorney, in July 2025. Goiás Civil Police (2025)
R$ 1 mnwas the value of the property in Aparecida de Goiânia that a couple tried to register with a forged power of attorney from another state, in March 2025. The QR code did not match the seal. Goiás Civil Police (2025)
2.26 mnfraud reports in Brazil in 2025, of all kinds, not only property. That is 429.8% more than in 2018. Brazilian Public Security Yearbook 2026, via BP Money

In these cases the check worked: the notary office got suspicious and called the police. But it came at the end, when the buyer had already trusted the paper. The notary office caught the problem, and the citizen was harmed anyway.

The tools already exist. They are hidden.

  1. 1
    QR code on the sealSince 2017, every notary act in Goiás can carry a code you check on your phone, for free.
    Catch: it is optional, and almost nobody knows it exists
  2. 2
    Central registry of powers of attorneySince July 2025, anyone can check whether a deed or power of attorney was really issued.
    Catch: it costs money and requires a digital certificate
  3. 3
    Online property recordThe registrars' platform shows a property's record right away.
    Catch: it costs money, has no legal value and asks for the record number, which the seller is the one who provides

For an elderly person who has never heard of a digital certificate, these tools do not exist.

What I propose: check before you pay

  1. 1
    One check, on gov.brThe buyer enters the seller's CPF (Brazil's tax ID) and the document code: property record, deed or power of attorney. The answer is simple and free: does this person own this property? Does this power of attorney exist and is it still valid? The check confirms, it does not list: nobody can find out which properties someone owns from their CPF alone.
  2. 2
    The seller answers for what they declareIf the data does not match, the deal is flagged as suspicious and the real owner gets an alert on gov.br.
  3. 3
    Protection at paymentA transfer above R$ 100 thousand made by an elderly person asks for the reason. If the reason is buying a property, the bank checks it through Open Finance against the same record. If it is proven that the seller is not the owner, the money does not leave.
  4. 4
    Tell everyoneExplain in plain language, on radio, TV, social media and at Vapt Vupt service centers, how to check a property before paying.

This is not a new, expensive system. It is a check on top of databases that already exist, using the same infrastructure as gov.br and Open Finance. Goiás can run the campaign and bring the check to Vapt Vupt. Making the registry, gov.br and the banks talk to each other takes a federal law and the Central Bank.

Chapter 7 · The criticism

The hardest questions

These are the toughest questions I'd expect to hear, and my answers.

Do you want to get rid of notary offices?

No. Nobody closes a notary office by decree. The proposal is a public counter that competes in the simple case, as Portugal did with Casa Pronta.

If the registry ever becomes state-run, the transition is gradual, as offices become vacant, with retraining paid by the fund for the people who work there. That's about 96,700 employees, according to the sector itself (Anoreg/BR, 2025).

An elderly person can be coerced into selling. Do biometrics solve that?

Biometrics prove who someone is, not that their will is free. That's why the simple path has a trained person who talks to the seller, with a specific questionnaire for elderly and vulnerable people.

Debts, liens and the spouse's signature show up on screen, because the system queries the official databases. Today it's the citizen who has to chase all that.

Gov.br biometrics have already been defrauded. Why trust them?

In 2025 the Federal Police broke up a group that bypassed gov.br's facial biometrics (Operation Face Off). Any system can be attacked, including today's: a property registry in Caxias do Sul already had all its data held for ransom.

The answer is layer upon layer: physical presence at the counter, checks against the official database, an interview and a history that can't be erased, with copies in separate places.

Deeds by video already exist. Isn't this reinventing the wheel?

They have existed since 2020, and that's progress. But the price is the same as at the counter, and you can only choose a notary from your state or the property's state.

Going digital without getting cheaper and without competition just moves the paper around.

There are hardly any property lawsuits. Why change it?

Security comes from the checking, not from the stamp. The simple path checks more data than a single person can, and in seconds.

What changes is the number of steps and the price, not the care.

Goiás can't legislate on property registration.

True: public registries are a federal matter (Constitution, art. 22). That's why the proposal separates what Goiás can do now, like the counter, the software and the 40% in transfers, from what needs federal law.

In Congress, the first concrete step is the law for the simple path for paid-off, debt-free property, plus publishing what each notary office collects.

Who pays for free birth certificates if prices fall?

Today they are paid for by a fund that receives 2.5% of notary fees in Goiás, FUNCOMP. That money remains guaranteed, in plain sight.

And birth registration already happens inside maternity hospitals, linked to registry offices. We can expand what works and bring it to Vapt Vupt.

Why print paper in a digital proposal?

Nobody reads today's A4 pages. The one-page summary is clear, good-looking and has a code to check in the official system.

It doesn't replace the registration. It explains it.

The 40% funds pay for the Judiciary. Isn't the problem the State itself?

Partly, yes. These transfers work like a tax hidden in the notary's price.

If the Judiciary, the Public Prosecutor's Office or the State Assembly need that money, let it be in the budget, in plain sight. And let the receipt say how much went where.

The notary office caught the scam. The system worked. Why change it?

The notary office caught the problem, but the citizen had already been harmed. In the case shown by Record, the first person arrested was the victim.

Government has a duty to show people the right ways to check and to make them simple. Checking at the end protects the registry. Checking before protects the person.

Elderly people don't use the gov.br app. Scams exploit trust, not a missing website.

That is why the protection does not depend only on them opening an app. A large transfer by an elderly person asks for the reason, and the bank checks that the recipient really owns the property before the money leaves.

Information comes with it: explaining in plain language, where people are, how to check before paying.

Looking up property owners by CPF helps scammers pick victims.

That concern deserves serious study. The proposed check confirms, it does not list: you already need the seller's CPF and the document number, and the answer is just yes or no.

Every deal has two sides. Whoever checks logs in through gov.br, and the seller answers for what they declare: wrong data becomes a suspicion alert.

Another expensive system. Who pays?

The databases already exist: the electronic registry, the powers of attorney registry, gov.br and Open Finance. The check is a link between them, and can share infrastructure with other government services.

Building software got much cheaper with artificial intelligence. What is expensive today is the scam.

Holding an elderly person's money takes away their freedom.

The safeguard does not decide for anyone. It only asks for the reason of a large transfer. Payment is blocked only when it is proven that the seller does not own the property.

Isn't someone who buys a farm without checking anything to blame?

Every case gets its own investigation. A good information system separates good intentions from bad ones: it protects whoever was deceived and punishes whoever deceived.

If checking were simple and free, the victim would have found the scam before paying.

Chapter 8 · How to measure

Four numbers to track

  • Total cost of transferring a property, as a % of its value, separating tax, notary and funds.
  • Steps and days from signed contract to finished registration.
  • Public revenue figures for each notary office, updated every year on the CNJ's Justiça Aberta dashboard.
  • Scams caught before payment, not only at registration, involving forged property documents.

How much did you pay to put your property in your name? And did you know where each part went?
