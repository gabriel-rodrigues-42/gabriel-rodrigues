A Brazilian platform that is already live

galera.club is a Brazilian link-in-bio platform. The page sells with Pix, sends the order ready to WhatsApp and charges nothing per sale. I built it, and it's running at galera.club.

See a real page

The problem

People who sell in Brazil sell through Instagram and WhatsApp. The link in bio is that person's storefront. And that storefront almost always belongs to a foreign company, charged in dollars and designed around credit cards.

On Linktree, for example, every sale pays 2.9% plus 30 US cents to Stripe, on any plan, on top of Linktree's own fee, which varies by plan.

Meanwhile, Brazil built Pix, the Central Bank's instant payment system. In 2025 it passed 80 billion transactions and moved more than R$ 35 trillion. It makes sense for the small Brazilian seller's storefront to revolve around it.

154 millionindividuals use Pix, 89% of adults Central Bank of Brazil, October 2026
R$ 35 tnmoved through Pix in 2025, up 33.8% Central Bank of Brazil, 2026
2.9% + US$ 0.30per sale to Stripe, on every Linktree plan Linktree help center, October 2026

How a sale happens

  1. The person sets up the page

    An address with their own name, with links, products and prices. The page serves Instagram and TikTok, it doesn't compete with them.

  2. The customer chooses and gets the Pix code

    The Pix code is generated on the page itself, following the Central Bank's BR Code standard, with the seller's key and amount. No payment company sits in between.

  3. The money lands straight in the seller's account

    The platform never touches the money and charges no commission. The only possible cost is whatever fee the seller's own bank charges.

  4. The order arrives ready

    It goes to WhatsApp with the message already written, or to the page's own chat, where a mascot answers the frequently asked questions the owner wrote. The owner chooses the channel.

  5. People in the city find it

    In the Market, pages show up by city. A regular link in bio is an island. Here the neighborhood finds who sells nearby.

It is already running

The numbers below come from galera.club's own code, at version 2.60.0, counted on November 19, 2026.

67 versionsreleased between August 22 and November 16, 2026
~220 thousandlines of TypeScript, across the app and the API
530 test files443 unit, 51 integration against a real database and 36 browser tests
71 tablesin PostgreSQL, with 129 reviewed migrations

Every release backs up the database first, checks the system's health afterwards and rolls back on its own to the previous version if something fails. Version 2.60.0 went live on November 16, 2026 through that pipeline.

  • Technology: Angular 22 with server-side rendering, Node.js with Express, PostgreSQL and Redis.
  • Security: mandatory second factor on every account, with a code on the phone or a passkey.
  • Real LGPD compliance: deleting the account deletes everything in a single operation, including reports and uploaded files. Analytics and marketing start switched off.
  • Languages: written in Portuguese, translated into English and Spanish.
  • Statistics: page visits and clicks for up to 90 days, for free.
  • Moderation: artificial intelligence only flags scams, hate and spam. People from the community decide, and no single person can take down anything from anyone.

What is already Brazilian and what isn't yet

Digital sovereignty is a big word. I'd rather show where galera.club really stands.

Already is

  • The payment: Pix, created and run by the Central Bank of Brazil.
  • The money: goes straight to the seller, with no platform commission.
  • The code and the decisions: made in Brazil, by a developer from Goiás.
  • The privacy rule: LGPD built into the code from the start.
  • The price: in reais, and the free plan has everything that helps you sell.

Not yet

  • Database backups live on Cloudflare, an American company.
  • Moderation uses foreign artificial intelligence models: Anthropic, Google and DeepSeek.
  • Orders can go through WhatsApp, which belongs to Meta. The page's own chat exists, but customers still live there.
  • The optional crypto payment (USDC on the Solana network) uses foreign infrastructure.

Why invest in this

The bridge to the Sovereign Fund for Reindustrialization

The first tier of the FSRI is precisely for digital platforms and infrastructure, such as a national cloud. The proposal mentions platforms integrated with Pix and Gov.br, the federal government's digital ID, charging micro-fees of 2% to 5%.

galera.club shows that this is possible, and that you can even charge zero per sale. To clear the "not yet" list, what's missing is money: hosting and storing backups in Brazil and using artificial intelligence models trained here.

A Brazilian platform doesn't need to beat the foreign one at everything. It needs to exist, work and have the stamina to grow.

The toughest criticism

Linktree already exists. Why one more?

Because the product is centered on the way Brazilians buy. Pix with no platform fee, orders on WhatsApp and discovery by city. In foreign tools, that's an add-on or doesn't exist.

Why would anyone pay, if WhatsApp is free?

Today nobody pays. Everything that helps you sell is free and will stay free. The planned paid plan sells looks and space, like removing the brand from the page. Never the tool for making money.

A finished platform with no users proves nothing.

True. The code is ready and live, what's missing is people. The next goal is to have many pages in a single city, starting with Goiânia, and to show the numbers in public.

How to measure whether it worked

Three numbers, which I plan to publish here: how many pages are active, how many cities have at least ten pages in the Market and how much money went through Pix straight to sellers, with no commission at all.
