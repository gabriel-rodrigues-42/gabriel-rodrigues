Simulator

Build your own FSRI portfolio

The FSRI is the fund I propose to bring industry and technology back to Brazil. Here you play investor: pick companies, follow the rules and see what could happen to your money.

The companies are made up. The FSRI does not exist yet. The names, sectors and numbers on each card are only there to show the rules.

The scenarios use what similar Brazilian funds returned from 1994 to 2022. This is not a promise of returns.

The FSRI's advantage is in the tax, not in the return. It was designed for people with very high incomes who would pay a lot of tax. For people who pay little tax, fixed income earns about the same with no risk.

Each company sits in a risk tier. Tier 1 pays back sooner. Tier 3 takes longer but has more insurance. In the proposal, government insurance covers part of the loss if the company fails.

The rules to get the tax exemption

    There is a fourth rule: the money stays invested for at least 5 years. That is why the shortest period in the simulation is 5 years.

    Step 3 of 3

    What could happen

    Each scenario shows what the money would become at the end of the period, after tax. The CDI (Brazil's interbank rate) is the benchmark for money left in the bank with no risk.

    13.65% is the CDI with the Selic policy rate at 13.75% (Central Bank, Nov 4, 2026). You can change it.

    What this result means

    In the average scenario, the FSRI ends up close to the CDI. The difference comes from the tax exemption, not from a higher return.

    For people who pay little tax, fixed income is still the right choice. The advantage shows up for people earning more than R$ 600,000 a year, who pay the minimum tax of up to 10% on their income (Law 15,270 of 2025). For them, the comparison is FSRI versus paying the tax.

    How the math works

    The average scenario uses the average of 387 Brazilian funds that buy stakes in companies: 12.7% a year, after fees. Between the best and the worst funds, the gap was 25.3 points a year. The bad and good scenarios sit half that gap below and above the average: about 0% and 25% a year.

    The same rate applies to the whole portfolio, because the study does not split funds by the FSRI tiers. If the rules are not met, the profit pays 15% tax, like the CDI after two years. With the rules met, the profit is tax-free.

    Insurance only pays if the company really goes bankrupt, confirmed by an audit. It does not cover profit that never came.
