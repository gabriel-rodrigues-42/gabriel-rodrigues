Idea

Cannabis already has rules: the dealers'.

Today, whoever sells cannabis doesn't ask for ID, doesn't say what they are selling and doesn't pay tax. The dealer decides who buys, what it costs and what it is mixed with.

In 2024, Brazil's Supreme Court (STF) ruled that possessing cannabis for personal use is not a crime. But selling still is, and there is nowhere legal to buy. This page shows what other countries learned and a two-part bill: hemp for industry and clubs for adults, with rules, an age limit and taxes.

Chapter 1 · How it works today

Users are out of criminal law, but sales are still in it

  1. 1
    Using has not meant prison since 2006The Drug Law replaced prison for users with a warning and a course (art. 28). Trafficking carries 5 to 15 years (art. 33). The law did not say what separates one from the other.
    Bottleneck: police and judges decide case by case
  2. 2
    In 2024, the Supreme Court set a numberAnyone carrying up to 40 grams or with up to six female plants is treated as a user, "until Congress legislates". The presumption falls if there are signs of sale, such as scales or packaging.
  3. 3
    Selling is still a crimeBuying for personal use is still prohibited, even though it is not a crime. Users leave the police station and go back to the same seller. He doesn't ask their age, doesn't state the potency and issues no receipt.
    Bottleneck: the money from every purchase goes to crime
  4. 4
    The line becomes a court caseWith no clear rule for sellers, the same amount can lead to a warning or a trafficking conviction, depending on the city and the judge.
    Bottleneck: costly prison terms that don't take drugs off the street
Chapter 2 · The numbers

Three numbers to grasp the scale

192,699people in cells for trafficking, 26% of the total, in the second half of 2025. Senappen, via Brasil de Fato (2026)
23% to 35%of trafficking defendants could have been considered users, depending on the criterion. Ipea (2024)
40 gis the amount up to which the Supreme Court presumes personal use, until Congress passes a law. STF, RE 635,659, Topic 506 (2024)

How much does crime earn from cannabis in Brazil? There is no official figure. That is why this page doesn't promise to end organized crime. It promises to take part of the market away from it.

Chapter 3 · How other countries do it

Each country got a different part wrong

I lived in Malta, which regulated cannabis in 2021. Countries follow three paths: commercial stores, state control and non-profit clubs. Each one teaches something.

Uruguay, 2013

One law for hemp and cannabis

Sales in pharmacies, clubs or home growing, with state registration and only for residents. Among students aged 13 to 17, past-year use fell from 19.0% in 2021 to 14.8% in 2024. Supply is short, and only 46% of users are registered to buy legally.

National Drug Board of Uruguay (2025 and 2026)

Canada, 2018

Started with hemp, 20 years earlier

Hemp has been legal since 1998. Adult use came in 2018, with licensed stores. After edibles were allowed, emergency visits by children under 10 in Ontario became 9 times more frequent.

Industrial Hemp Regulations (1998); Myran et al., JAMA Network Open (2022)

Malta, 2021

The Spanish club, with a law

Non-profit associations of up to 500 members, residents only, with a quota per person and weaker products for under-21s. It works, but grows slowly: there were 22 associations in February 2026.

Maltese authority (ARUC), via trade press (2026)

Spain

Clubs without a law become a lottery

Clubs live off court rulings, with no national law. The Constitutional Court struck down the laws of Navarre (2017) and Catalonia (2018). Each judge decides differently, and in Barcelona clubs turned into sales points for tourists.

Público (2023); Constitutional Court, STC 144/2017 and 100/2018

Germany, 2024

Clubs alone are not enough

By 31 October 2025, Germany had 366 approved growing associations. They supplied at most 3.5% of users. The rest still buy from acquaintances, grow at home or use the illegal market.

EKOCAN, second evaluation report (2026)

Thailand, 2022

Freed it with no law, then backtracked

It took cannabis off the drug list without any control rules. It reached 18,433 sales outlets by the end of 2025, many aimed at tourists. In 2025 it started requiring a prescription, and in 2026 the government sent Parliament a bill returning to medical use only.

Forbes (2026)

Chapter 4 · What I propose

One law, two parts, the same cooperative

Part 1 · Hemp for industry

Hemp is the same plant, from varieties with almost no THC (up to 0.3%). It doesn't get you high. It becomes fabric, paper, food, animal feed and building material.

  • Today only authorized companies may grow hemp in Brazil, and Anvisa's 2026 rules cover medical use only (STJ, 2024; Anvisa, 2026). The law opens the way for fiber, construction and food.
  • Growers are licensed cooperatives and small farmers. In Brazil, a cooperative is already, by law, a society without profit as its goal.
  • THC is measured in the final product, not just in the plant. The US measured only the plant, saw products that get you high sold as "hemp", and had to change the law in 2025.
Part 2 · Clubs for adults
  • Only in non-profit clubs. Members pay a monthly fee and for the product. The club pays no profit to an owner. The government collects tax and license fees.
  • From age 21, with ID. From 21 to 25, a smaller quota and a THC cap. In Quebec, after the age went up to 21, past-3-month use among people aged 18 to 20 rose 16.4 percentage points less than in the rest of Canada.
  • The Supreme Court's 40 g reference stands. The law creates no lower limit that would leave users worse off than today.
  • Every gram is tracked, by batch, from the cooperative's field to the club. The club's cash has to match the grams that went out. That is what shuts the door on money laundering.
Tourists in two phases
  • For the first 3 years, residents only: Brazilians and foreigners who live here. Uruguay, Malta and Germany do this.
  • After a public evaluation, each city decides whether to accept tourists. They buy with a passport, with a smaller quota, and the club checks the document without keeping any data.
Chapter 5 · Protection

Rules on the product, not on the police

Checking every person who leaves a club is impossible. So protection starts with the packaging and at the counter.

People who don't use

Plain, child-proof packaging, with a seal stamped on every piece. Edibles that don't look like regular sweets, with a THC cap per pack. No advertising: in clubs, only educational posters and videos. Use on the street: a warning first, then a small fine.

People who use

Members pay by Pix or card, the receipt has no taxpayer ID and the charge shows a neutral name. The government knows how much was sold, but banks don't build a list of who bought. Legal use outside working hours is added to the law against discriminatory dismissal.

For professional drivers, nothing changes. The mandatory drug test for C, D and E licences stays as it is, and a positive result suspends the right to drive for 3 months (Traffic Code, art. 148-A). The warning goes on the packaging.

Chapter 6 · The money

It goes back to society, with open books

The Constitution forbids tying tax revenue to a body or an expense (art. 167, IV). That's why the proposal creates a sector contribution of its own, with three rules:

  • The money goes into a fund with accounts open online, a yearly audit by the Federal Court of Accounts (TCU) and a report on where every real went.
  • It pays for programs with deadlines and targets: health information in the public health system (SUS) for users and families, and technical training for police to investigate crimes on the street and online.
  • It doesn't pay fixed salaries. Revenue falls over time: in Colorado, it went from US$ 424 million in 2020-21 to US$ 231 million in 2024-25.

Hemp is industry

Cooperatives growing hemp and factories turning it into fabric and building material is reindustrialization in the countryside. That's why Part 1 of this law becomes a project of the Sovereign Fund for Reindustrialization and Innovation. At the launch of a report on hemp by Embrapa and Instituto Ficus in 2025, Instituto Escolhas estimated R$ 5.76 billion in revenue and over 14,000 jobs by 2030, if rules are in place. It is an estimate.

Chapter 7 · The criticism

The hardest questions

These are the toughest questions I would expect to hear, and my answers.

So you want to free up drugs?

No. The Supreme Court already took possession out of criminal law in 2024. What's left is deciding who sells: organized crime, with no rules at all, or an inspected club, with an age limit, a label and taxes.

Young people will use more.

That's why the age is 21, with no advertising and a THC cap until 25. The evidence from abroad is mixed. In Uruguay and Colorado, student use fell. A 2024 review found slightly higher odds of youth use where cannabis was legalized (JAMA Pediatrics). The law has to measure this every year.

Clubs can't supply everyone.

True, and Germany showed it: 3.5%. The law needs fast licensing for cooperatives and clubs, and a public evaluation after 3 years to correct course.

Most Brazilians are against it.

In March 2024, 67% were against even decriminalizing possession (Datafolha). This is an idea for debate, not a finished bill. I want to show that this topic can be discussed with data and rules, and to hear from people who disagree.

Brazil signed the UN drug conventions.

So did Uruguay and Canada, and they regulated anyway. The final text needs a legal opinion on this before it becomes a bill.

What if the amendment criminalizing any amount passes?

Constitutional amendment PEC 45/2023 has already passed the Senate. If it becomes part of the Constitution, no ordinary law can regulate adult use. The hemp-for-industry part would still be possible.

Chapter 8 · How to measure it

Five numbers to track

  • People jailed for trafficking small amounts, per year (Senappen).
  • Use among students aged 13 to 17 (IBGE national school survey).
  • Child poisonings (public health system reports).
  • Share of consumption bought at clubs, by yearly survey.
  • Hemp area planted and jobs at cooperatives.
