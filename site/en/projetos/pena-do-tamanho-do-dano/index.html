Idea

Every R$ 690 stolen is the Bolsa Família of one family for a month.

That was the average payment in February 2026, to 18.84 million families (Ministry of Development and Social Assistance). Bolsa Família is Brazil's cash transfer program for low-income families. Measuring corruption this way helps everyone grasp the size of the harm.

The idea started simple: every Bolsa Família payment stolen means one more month in prison. I did the math and it breaks down early. This page shows where it breaks, how other countries do it, and a version that keeps the spirit of the idea.

Reference payment: R$ 690.01 (Ministry of Development and Social Assistance, February 2026). 40-year cap: Penal Code, art. 75. Under any rule, people who do not pay the money back stay in closed prison (art. 33, § 4). The calculator is a simulation for debate, not a sentence.

Chapter 2 · How it works today

The law already punishes, but the amount counts for little and loosely

  1. 1
    The crime carries 2 to 12 yearsIt applies to whoever steals public money (embezzlement), to the public official who takes a bribe and to whoever offers it. Stealing R$ 5 thousand or R$ 500 million makes no difference: the range is the same.
    Bottleneck: the range does not grow with the harm
  2. 2
    The judge works out the sentenceThey look at eight points, such as guilt, motives and the "consequences of the crime" (Penal Code, art. 59). The harm comes in there, but each judge decides how much it weighs.
    Bottleneck: similar cases can get very different sentences
  3. 3
    No repayment, no way out of closed prisonIn crimes against public administration, the prisoner only moves to a semi-open regime after paying back what was stolen (art. 33, § 4). The judge can also seize assets the person cannot explain (art. 91-A).
  4. 4
    Outside criminal courtThe Administrative Misconduct Act recovers the money, removes the official from office and suspends political rights, but does not lead to prison. The Anti-Corruption Act fines companies up to 20% of revenue.
    Bottleneck: long cases and the risk of the statute of limitations running out

40 yearsis the most anyone can serve in prison in Brazil. It used to be 30. Penal Code, art. 75 (Law 13,964/2019)

How much does Brazil lose to corruption each year? There is no official number. The most quoted estimates come from private groups, such as the São Paulo industry federation (Fiesp), and use perception indexes, not traced money. That is why they are not presented here as fact.

Chapter 4 · Where the straight math breaks

At one month per payment, everyone hits the cap

There are 480 months in 40 years. At R$ 690.01 a month, the math reaches the cap at R$ 331 thousand stolen. From there on, stealing R$ 400 thousand and stealing R$ 1 billion get the same sentence.

There is another problem. Long sentences deter less than they seem to. The US National Institute of Justice sums up the research this way: the chance of being caught deters far more than the size of the punishment (NIJ, 2016). In Brazil, what is most missing is fast trials and keeping cases from expiring.

Chapter 5 · How other countries do it

Countries that look at the amount use steps, not a straight line

United States

A table with 16 steps

In federal fraud, theft and bribery cases, the judge adds points according to the loss. Each step needs two to three times more money than the one before. The table was updated on November 1, 2026.

  1. more than US$ 9 thousand+2 points
  2. more than US$ 150 thousand+8
  3. more than US$ 2 million+16
  4. more than US$ 35 million+22
  5. more than US$ 750 million+30

U.S. Sentencing Commission, amendment 838 to § 2B1.1 (2026)

Germany

A large benefit, a longer sentence

An official who takes a bribe gets 6 months to 5 years. If the benefit is "of great magnitude", the case becomes especially serious and the sentence goes from 1 to 10 years. The law does not set the amount: the courts decide.

German Criminal Code, §§ 332 and 335

China and Vietnam

Ranges by amount that reach the death penalty

In China, since 2016, anyone who embezzles or takes 3 million yuan or more can be sentenced to death. Vietnam also has ranges by amount and sentenced a businesswoman to death in 2024. In Brazil, the Constitution forbids the death penalty and life imprisonment (art. 5, XLVII). That path does not work here.

Time, 2016; Fortune, 2024

The US model is the closest to the idea. It works because it grows slowly: the money has to multiply for the sentence to go up one step. That keeps the cap for the enormous cases.

Chapter 6 · What I propose

Measure in Bolsa Família payments and raise the sentence in steps

  1. 1
    The ruling shows the harm in Bolsa Família paymentsEvery conviction for stealing public money states how many payments that amount would cover, using last year's average. The number goes on the Transparency Portal. It does not change the sentence, but everyone understands the size of the case.
  2. 2
    The sentence goes up one step for every 10 times more moneyUp to about R$ 69 thousand (100 payments), 2 to 4 years. Every 10 times more, 2 more years. Above R$ 690 million, a new range of 12 to 14 years, and 14 to 16 above R$ 6.9 billion. The judge still chooses within each step.
  3. 3
    One third more if the money was for health, education or social assistanceThese are the services low-income families depend on most. Today the law does not care where the money came from.
  4. 4
    Paying back still pays offWhoever pays it all back before being charged gets a shorter sentence, as today. Whoever does not pay back stays in closed prison. What is recovered goes to social assistance.
  5. 5
    Deadlines for trialsCases of stolen public money move up the line, with a deadline for each phase. A longer sentence is useless if the case expires before the ruling.

The path is a bill that changes the Penal Code (arts. 312, 317, 327 and 333) and the Code of Criminal Procedure. The steps in reais would be updated every year by the average Bolsa Família payment.

Chapter 7 · The criticism

The hardest questions

These are the toughest questions I would expect to hear, and my answers.

Longer sentences do not solve anything. Brazil's problem is impunity.

I agree that certainty of punishment matters more. That is why the proposal has trial deadlines and keeps repayment as the condition to leave closed prison.

The steps are not about filling prisons. They are so that stealing R$ 500 million does not fit the same range as stealing R$ 5 thousand.

Why measure in Bolsa Família? That mixes two subjects.

It is the best-known measure of how much money keeps a family going for a month. The stolen money and the payment come from the same place: the public budget.

It could be the minimum wage. What matters is swapping "millions" for something people can picture.

This takes away the judge's freedom and could be struck down by the Supreme Court.

Automatic math would be struck down. The Constitution requires individual sentencing (art. 5, XLVI), and the Supreme Court has already struck down a sentence out of proportion with the crime (RE 979,962, 2021).

That is why the proposal uses steps with ranges. The judge still decides within each one, as today.

With bribes, which amount counts? The bribe can be small and the loss huge.

The larger of the two counts: the bribe or the loss the contract caused to public money. The loss has to be proven in court, with an expert report or an audit by the audit court.

Keeping someone in prison is expensive for the state.

It is. That is why the focus is still on getting the money back and seizing assets. Long sentences are for cases with enormous amounts, which are few.

This will only catch the small fish.

The steps do the opposite. Up to R$ 69 thousand, the sentence starts at 2 years, as today. The increase shows up in the large amounts.

Chapter 8 · How to measure it

Four numbers to track

  • Time to ruling: how many months a case of stolen public money takes, from charges to trial.
  • Money returned: what share of the stolen amount goes back to public coffers, case by case.
  • Expired cases: how many cases die without a trial.
  • Sentences by step: whether cases with similar amounts get similar sentences.

When you hear that R$ 10 million was stolen, can you picture the size? What if they said 14 thousand Bolsa Família payments?
