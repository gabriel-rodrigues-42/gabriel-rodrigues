Privacy

What this site keeps about you

Summary: I keep what you type in the "Do you think I should try?" form. If you accept, I also count visits with Google Analytics. I don't use ads.

This is a translation for convenience. If anything differs, the Portuguese version is the one that applies.

Who looks after the data

Gabriel Rodrigues, as an individual, owner of this site. Contact for any privacy matter: gabrielr47@gmail.com.

What data and what for

  • Email: to send news about my preparation and the ideas on this site.
  • Name, city and state (optional): to address you by name and know where the people following along come from.
  • Date and time of the authorization: to prove you gave consent.

I don't ask for CPF (Brazilian tax ID), voter ID, phone number or address. I also don't store your IP address with your sign-up.

Why I ask for your authorization

Saying you support my attempt reveals a political opinion. Brazil's data protection law, the LGPD, treats this as sensitive data (Law 13.709/2018, art. 5, II). That's why I only keep it with your specific and highlighted consent (art. 11, I), in the box you tick on the form.

This sign-up is not a request for votes, not party membership and not a petition.

Who I share it with

Nobody. I don't sell, lend or trade the list. Brazilian electoral law also forbids selling email lists (Law 9.504/1997, art. 57-E).

The data is stored in Cloudflare's database (Cloudflare D1), which hosts this site. The servers may be outside Brazil.

For how long

Until you ask to be removed. If I give up on the project, I delete the list.

If I ever become a candidate, I'll only send messages to people who signed up here, always with a way to unsubscribe in every email, as electoral law requires (Law 9.504/1997, arts. 57-B and 57-G).

Location and CEP in the city field

The city field (in the form and in the Which path questionnaire) has two shortcuts. They only work when you click them.

  • Use my location: the browser asks for your permission. If you allow it, I send the approximate coordinates to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, only to find out the name of the city.
  • Search by CEP (Brazilian postal code): I send the CEP to ViaCEP (or to BrasilAPI, if ViaCEP fails), only to find out the city.

This site doesn't keep coordinates or CEP. The form only keeps what you see in the field: city and state. These services receive your IP address, like any site the browser visits.

Slogan vote

On the home page you can choose between two phrases for my slogan. The vote is anonymous: I only keep how many votes each phrase has. I don't keep your name, email or IP address with the vote.

To count one vote per device, your browser remembers the phrase you chose (in local storage, not a cookie). This stays only on your device.

To stop bots, I count how many votes each network sends per day. For that, the IP address becomes a scrambled code (a hash) made with a random key that changes every day. The next day, the key and the codes are deleted, and nobody can link the code back to the IP address.

Cookies and visit measurement

When you enter the site, a notice asks whether I can measure visits. Until you accept, the site loads nothing from Google.

If you accept, I load Google Tag Manager and Google Analytics. They use cookies to count pages viewed, where the visit came from, the type of device and the approximate city. I use this only to know which ideas interest people most. Advertising cookies are always off.

Legal basis: your consent (LGPD, art. 7, I). Google processes this data and may store it outside Brazil.

You can change your mind at any time through the "Cookies" link in the footer of every page. Your choice is saved only in your browser.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete your data, or withdraw your authorization, at any time (LGPD, art. 18). Write to gabrielr47@gmail.com from the same email you signed up with. I reply within 15 days.

If you think I didn't take good care of your data, you can also file a complaint with the ANPD, Brazil's data protection authority.
